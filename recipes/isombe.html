Rwanda · East Africa

Isombe

Cassava leaves pounded and simmered for hours with peanut paste, eggplant and palm oil into a rich, deep-green stew. A staple of Rwandan home cooking.

Prep
30 min
Cook
2 hr
Serves
4
Level
Easy
Jump to recipe ↓

The story

Rwanda's everyday green

Cassava came to Africa from South America in the 1500s and spread across the continent. Rwandans eat not only the root but also the young leaves, which are pounded in a wooden mortar until finely shredded.

Raw cassava leaves contain natural compounds that must be broken down, which is why isombe is pounded and then simmered for a long time. The finished stew is enriched with peanut paste and palm oil, and often with eggplant, spinach, onion and leeks.

Isombe is served with rice, ugali, boiled plantain or sweet potatoes, and it appears at family meals and celebrations alike. The same dish is called sombe in Burundi and pondu or saka-saka in the Congo.

You will need

Ingredients

  • Pounded cassava leaves (fresh or frozen) 500 g
  • Water 1 L
  • Onion, chopped 1
  • Leek or spring onions, sliced 1
  • Garlic 2 cloves
  • Eggplant, diced 1
  • Spinach 100 g
  • Unsweetened peanut paste 4 tbsp
  • Red palm oil 3 tbsp
  • Stock cube (optional) 1
  • Salt To taste

Step by step

Method

01 · Prepare the leaves
  1. If using fresh leaves, remove the stems, wash well and pound in a mortar (or pulse in a food processor) until finely shredded.
  2. Frozen pounded leaves can go straight into the pot.
02 · Boil
  1. Put the leaves and water in a large pot and bring to the boil.
  2. Simmer uncovered for 1 hour, stirring now and then and adding water so the leaves stay covered.
03 · Add the vegetables
  1. Add the onion, leek, garlic, eggplant and spinach and simmer for 30 minutes more.
04 · Enrich
  1. Loosen the peanut paste with a ladle of the cooking liquid, then stir it in with the palm oil and stock cube.
  2. Simmer on low heat for 20–30 minutes, stirring so it does not stick, until thick and glossy. Season with salt.
05 · Serve
  1. Serve hot with rice, ugali, boiled green plantain or sweet potatoes.

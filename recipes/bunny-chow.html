Bunny Chow

A quarter loaf of white bread, hollowed out and filled with spicy Durban curry. The bread you scoop out becomes the lid, and your spoon.

Prep
20 min
Cook
1 hr 15 min
Serves
4
Level
Easy
Jump to recipe ↓

The story

Durban in a loaf

Bunny chow was born in Durban, home to one of the largest Indian communities outside India. Many families descend from indentured laborers who were brought from India from 1860 onwards to work on the sugar plantations of Natal.

Its exact origin is debated. A popular story says Indian eateries began serving curry inside a hollowed-out loaf when segregation laws kept Black workers out of many restaurants: the bread made a container that could be carried away and eaten anywhere.

A proper Durban curry is fiery and red with masala, chili and curry leaves, and is made with mutton, chicken or sugar beans. Locals order a “quarter”, “half” or “full” bunny and eat it with their hands.

You will need

Ingredients

  • Mutton or lamb, cubed (or chicken thighs) 800 g
  • Vegetable oil 3 tbsp
  • Cinnamon stick 1
  • Cardamom pods 3
  • Fennel seeds 1 tsp
  • Onion, chopped 1
  • Curry leaves 1 sprig
  • Garlic and ginger paste 1 tbsp
  • Durban masala or hot curry powder 2 tbsp
  • Ground turmeric 0.5 tsp
  • Tomatoes, grated 2
  • Potatoes, quartered 3
  • Water 500 ml
  • Fresh coriander 1 handful
  • Unsliced white sandwich loaf 1
  • Salt To taste

Step by step

Method

01 · Temper the spices
  1. Heat the oil and fry the cinnamon, cardamom and fennel seeds for 1 minute.
  2. Add the onion and curry leaves and cook for about 8 minutes, until golden.
02 · Build the masala
  1. Stir in the garlic-ginger paste, masala and turmeric and cook for 1 minute, adding a splash of water if it catches.
  2. Add the tomatoes and cook for 5 minutes into a thick paste.
03 · Simmer the curry
  1. Add the meat, coat it in the masala and cook for 5 minutes. Pour in the water, cover and simmer for 45 minutes.
  2. Add the potatoes and simmer for 20–25 minutes more, until the meat is tender and the gravy is thick. Season and stir in the coriander.
04 · Prepare the bread
  1. Cut the loaf into four quarters. Scoop out the soft center of each piece, leaving a wall about 1 cm thick, and keep the scooped-out bread.
05 · Fill and serve
  1. Ladle the curry into the bread, top with the scooped-out bread as a lid and serve with a carrot, onion and chili sambal.

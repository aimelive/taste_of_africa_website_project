Jollof Rice

Long-grain rice cooked in a rich tomato and pepper stew until every grain turns orange-red, with a smoky layer at the bottom of the pot.

Prep
20 min
Cook
1 hr
Serves
4
Level
Medium
Jump to recipe ↓

The story

Born in the Wolof Empire

Jollof rice takes its name from the Wolof (or Jolof) Empire, which ruled parts of present-day Senegal and The Gambia from the 14th to the 16th century. The Senegalese dish thiéboudienne, rice cooked in a tomato and fish broth, is widely seen as its ancestor.

Today jollof is cooked across West Africa, and the friendly rivalry over who makes it best, the “jollof wars”, runs hottest between Nigeria and Ghana. Nigerian cooks build the flavor on a blended base of tomatoes, red bell peppers and scotch bonnet, fried in oil with curry powder, thyme and bay leaves.

Party jollof is cooked in huge pots over firewood, and the smoky taste from the slightly scorched bottom layer is exactly what guests hope for.

You will need

Ingredients

  • Long-grain parboiled rice 400 g
  • Plum tomatoes 4
  • Red bell peppers 2
  • Scotch bonnet pepper 1
  • Onions (1 blended, 1 sliced) 2
  • Vegetable oil 80 ml
  • Tomato paste 2 tbsp
  • Chicken stock 500 ml
  • Curry powder 1 tsp
  • Dried thyme 1 tsp
  • Bay leaves 2
  • Butter 1 tbsp
  • Salt To taste

Step by step

Method

01 · Make the pepper base
  1. Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth.
  2. Boil the purée in an uncovered pot for 15–20 minutes, until it is thick and reduced by about half.
02 · Fry the stew
  1. Heat the oil and fry the sliced onion until soft. Add the tomato paste and fry for 3–4 minutes until it darkens.
  2. Add the reduced pepper purée, curry powder, thyme and bay leaves. Fry for about 10 minutes, stirring, until the oil rises to the top.
03 · Cook the rice
  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then stir it into the stew with the stock. Season with salt.
  2. The liquid should just cover the rice. Cover the pot tightly with foil and a lid and cook on low heat for 30 minutes without stirring.
  3. If the rice is still firm, sprinkle in a little water, cover and cook for 5 minutes more.
04 · Finish and rest
  1. Turn up the heat for the last 2–3 minutes to toast the bottom layer for that smoky party flavor.
  2. Stir in the butter, rest for 5 minutes, then fluff and serve with fried plantain and chicken.

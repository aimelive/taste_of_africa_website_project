Morocco · North Africa

Lamb Tagine

Lamb shoulder slow-braised with ras el hanout, apricots and preserved lemon until it falls apart, served from the cone-lidded pot it is named after.

Prep
25 min
Cook
2 hr 15 min
Serves
4
Level
Easy
Jump to recipe ↓

The story

A pot and a dish

A tagine is both a dish and the pot it is cooked in: a shallow clay base with a tall, cone-shaped lid. Steam rises into the cone, cools and drips back down, so the meat braises slowly in very little liquid.

The cooking style goes back to the Amazigh (Berber) peoples of North Africa. Over centuries, Arab, Andalusian and Ottoman influences added sweet-and-savory pairings such as lamb with apricots, prunes or dates, and spice blends like ras el hanout, which means “head of the shop”: the best mix a spice seller has.

Moroccan cooks finish many tagines with preserved lemon and olives, and the whole table shares from one pot, scooping up the sauce with bread.

You will need

Ingredients

  • Lamb shoulder, cut into chunks 1 kg
  • Olive oil 3 tbsp
  • Onions, sliced 2
  • Garlic 3 cloves
  • Fresh ginger, grated 1 tbsp
  • Ras el hanout 2 tsp
  • Ground cumin 1 tsp
  • Ground cinnamon 1 tsp
  • Saffron threads 1 pinch
  • Stock (lamb or chicken) 500 ml
  • Dried apricots 150 g
  • Honey 1 tbsp
  • Preserved lemon 1
  • Toasted flaked almonds 30 g
  • Fresh coriander 1 handful
  • Salt and black pepper To taste

Step by step

Method

01 · Brown the lamb
  1. Season the lamb with salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a tagine or heavy casserole pot.
  2. Brown the lamb in batches for about 5 minutes per batch, then set it aside.
02 · Build the spice base
  1. Lower the heat and cook the onions for 8 minutes until soft and golden.
  2. Add the garlic, ginger, ras el hanout, cumin and cinnamon and cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
03 · Slow braise
  1. Return the lamb to the pot with the stock and saffron.
  2. Cover and cook on the lowest heat, or in a 160 °C oven, for 1½ hours. Use a heat diffuser if you cook a clay tagine on the stove.
04 · Add the fruit
  1. Stir in the apricots, honey and the chopped rind of the preserved lemon.
  2. Cook uncovered for 30–45 minutes, until the lamb is very tender and the sauce is thick and glossy.
05 · Serve
  1. Scatter over the almonds and coriander and serve with couscous or round Moroccan khobz bread.
